# Polymath Capstone resources

## Must read

- Your own module notes and gates, from P0 to P9 - the capstone is assembled from those
  artefacts, and the acceptance criteria are the index you answer against.
  https://github.com/aswathas/ai-engineer-polymath
- *The Staff Engineer's Path*, Tanya Reilly - the chapters on writing a design document
  and on scoping are the closest thing to a guide for the capstone's spec and bundle.
- *Style: Lessons in Clarity and Grace*, Amy Style - short, and it fixes the vague
  sentence that makes a spec impossible to review.
- The postmortem culture material used by public engineering organisations - the
  retrospective section of the capstone follows the same discipline: what happened, why,
  what changes, by when.

## Watch

- Recorded walkthroughs of real, shipped AI features - not the demos, the ones with the
  architecture and the trade-offs shown. Watch for how they describe the seams.
- The talks on AI product launches and their post-launch reviews - these map directly
  onto the rollout plan and the failure message.
- The retrospective formats used by teams that ship weekly - short, dated, and specific
  about the surprise.

## Skip

- New frameworks, new model providers and new papers - the capstone is an assembly of
  what you already built, not a place to try something new.
- Feature requests and scope expansion - if the feature needs a second idea to be
  complete, it is two features and it should be scoped down.
- Anything that makes the bundle prettier without making it truer - a rubric that
  measures the artefact is the only one that counts here.
